# ELEVATE Learner Evaluation Plan

This plan measures learning with the Know → Do → Become framework. It does not contain participant responses. No learning gain can be claimed until real learners complete the instruments below.

## Purpose

ELEVATE is an educator-led experiential platform organised as Explore → Learn → Experience → Validate → Apply → Transform. The evaluation asks whether a learner, after a Growth Journey:

- understands the journey concepts more clearly than before (Know),
- can produce practical work that an educator judges against a rubric (Do),
- and can describe a change in confidence, behaviour, and portfolio evidence (Become).

## Participants

Use learners who enrol voluntarily and give consent for their anonymised responses to be used in the project report. Do not use production accounts for destructive tests. Do not invent responses.

Minimum useful sample for a final-year demonstration: one intact learner cohort on one published journey, with the same educator marking every submission. A larger sample is preferable. Report the actual count, not a target, in the results section.

## KNOW — Pre-assessment questionnaire

Administer before lesson capsules begin. Use the same items again after the journey. Score each item 1 (not at all) to 5 (confidently).

1. I can explain the main problem this journey addresses.
2. I can name the core concepts required to solve that problem.
3. I can describe how those concepts connect to a practical task.
4. I can identify what “good work” looks like for this journey.
5. I know which stage of Explore, Learn, Experience, Validate, Apply, and Transform I am in.

Add three short-answer items written by the journey author, each tied to one concept taught in the capsules. Mark short answers with a simple key: 0 incorrect, 1 partial, 2 correct. Record the marker.

## KNOW — Post-assessment questionnaire

Repeat the same five rating items and the same three short-answer items after the learner has completed the required capsules and received a final submission decision. Do not change the wording between pre and post.

## DO — Practical performance rubric

The educator marks the practical submission. Scores are 1 to 4.

| Criterion | 1 — Beginning | 2 — Developing | 3 — Proficient | 4 — Advanced |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Problem understanding | Misses the task | Partial reading of the task | Addresses the task | Addresses the task and its constraints |
| Application of concepts | Concepts are absent or incorrect | Concepts are named but weakly applied | Concepts are applied correctly | Concepts are applied and justified |
| Artefact quality | Incomplete or not runnable / not usable | Works with major gaps | Meets the stated deliverable | Meets the deliverable and is clear to review |
| Response to feedback | Feedback is ignored | Some feedback is addressed | Requested revisions are addressed | Revisions improve the work beyond the request |

Record the first submission score and, if revision is requested, the revised submission score. The platform status must match the decision: `revision_requested` or `approved`.

## BECOME — Learner feedback questionnaire

Administer after the final decision. Ratings are 1 to 5.

1. I am more confident attempting this kind of task than I was at the start.
2. I changed how I approach the work because of educator feedback.
3. I can point to a concrete artefact in my portfolio that shows this change.
4. The six-stage journey helped me see what to do next.
5. I would recommend this journey to a peer, and I can say why.

Add one open response: “What changed for you between the first submission and the final decision?” Quote learners only with consent. Do not paraphrase a quote into a result the learner did not state.

## Analysis methodology

1. Include only rows with consent and a completed pre-assessment.
2. Report sample size, how many finished, and how many dropped out.
3. For Know, report the median pre score, median post score, and how many learners increased, stayed the same, or decreased. A paired comparison is appropriate if the sample is large enough to support it. With a very small cohort, report each anonymous row and do not claim statistical significance.
4. For Do, report the distribution of final rubric totals and how many revised submissions scored higher than the first.
5. For Become, report the median feedback total and the count of learners who linked a portfolio artefact. Treat open responses as illustrations, not as a measured effect.
6. Do not fill missing post scores with averages.
7. Do not describe the platform as effective until this table contains real rows.

## What this plan does not do

It does not replace educator judgement, and it does not treat certificate issuance as proof of learning. A certificate records that the platform’s completion rules were met. Learning claims require the instruments above.
